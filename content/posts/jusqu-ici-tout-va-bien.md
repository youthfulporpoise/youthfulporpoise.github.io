I like films, you like films. We all like films. (Sad fact: there are some who dont’t,
yikes.) Some films stay with us. Some stay longer. Some stay forever. I have quite a few
of them, as we all would.

*La Haine* is a ‘95 film by Mathieu Kassovitz starring Vincent Cassel.  It’s b/w, a
stylistic choice, and it’s rather short, running just about an hour and a half.  The
films begins with a joke about a falling man who keeps telling himself: *jusqu’ici tout
va bien.* So far, so good. So far, so good. Well, that’s balantly absurd, isn’t it?
Everything is good, until what? Until you land.

If you squint a bit and stretch the fall metaphorically, you’ve some insights on your
hands.

Let’s see. You’re falling. And you can see the ground getting closer and closer. But not
yet. You’re still safe. You simply don’t like thought that you’re not always going to be
safe, so you turn your back. What happens now? You fall on your back, surprise, surprise.
You should’ve seen it coming. Yet you did however.

At every instant through the fall, it’s comfier to do nothing falling free, than to do
something. At the instant you land, everything’s better than the inevitable meeting with
the ground. It’s a fashion of fear. It’s the fear of living. It’s the fear of meeting
the world as it is, not as you want it to be.

Yes. The world is fearsome. Every memorable act of your life appears threatening. Life
isn’t a maintenance of status quo. Any story unfolds only as it changes, more
exhilarating as it leaps. No, not a leap of faith, that isn’t what you should do. Faith
is nothing more than a pretty doll. These are leaps of surrender, at mercy to the
eventuality of everything intermingling to reach wherever it reaches. It’s more
significant that you leap than where you reach leaping. There’s no story in stasis. The
more there’s changing, the more there’s of the story.

The moral? Learn to risk comfort.
