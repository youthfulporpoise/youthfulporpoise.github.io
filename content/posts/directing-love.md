The language we have is both necessary and inadequate to describe our feelings towards
another. Take Derrida’s example. When I say “I love you” is the love the love of
*someone* or is it the love of *something*, that is am I referring to love as the love
toward that person absolute or am I referring to love as the love toward the qualities of
that person, their attributes, their idiosyncrasies, that they possess? Do I love that
person because she possesses those things, or do I love the person itself?  This is the
ambiguity regarding love (ignoring the fact that the English language uses *love* for a
lot of things, it is spread too thin; this is universal and regardless of any specific
language, because every language has a word for love i.e. snēham, not kāmam, pranayam,
ishtam, but snēham). Do I love her because she’s pretty or do I just simply love her? Do
I love her because she’s witty and intelligent or do I just simply love her?  Do I love
her because she reminds of me of someone I hold dear or do I just simply love her?

Let’s study the anatomy of being love-struck (for instance). This is how that probably
goes. You catch someone, you speak to someone, or you hear about someone, and then you’re
just enamoured with them. You can’t stop thinking about them. You can’t sleep, you can’t
study, you can’t do errands, you can’t think anything else even. We have a name for it.
Limerence. You suffer it as much as you enjoy it. Well yes, limerence is transcient and
some argue it’s not “real love” because it eventually fades; that’s not our point here.

See the thing is, you haven’t known them for that long, you haven’t even known them that
much, yet you burn, you pine, you perish. Now, do you love someone that they are or do
you love that someone because of the way they are? This is different from what most would
describe as that you’re loving an “ideal” of that person, not the person itself,
entiendes? You could perhaps apply here the analogy that the finger pointing at the moon
isn’t the moon, but that isn’t quite right also.

Do we ever really love someone?
