title: 'Main Character Syndrome'
private: true
---

I don’t know whether we somehow acquired it early on or if it came to us naturally, we
tend to narrate our life in chapters and episodes, delineated perfectly, each purportedly
so neatly never influencing the other. How come? A bad day is told to be simply an
insignificant hindrance in an arc of a self-assumed journey to greater things. And good
things? Weren’t they supposed to happen? That the journey is towards greater ambitions is
already presumed in the void of all foundations. We’ve become in our credulous minds the
lofty centre around which every person, matter, phenomenon, and moment revolves around.
We’ve bestowed us an undue signficance, a desperate sense of entitlement. And doesn’t it
seem so silly and childish from this vantage?

I know the feeling. Of course, it feels nice to go on about yourself as some hero in a
classical epic, or to subconsciously internalize the thought that everything is somehow
tied to you, your existence by mere existence subtly influencing everything. Or less
lame, that you can’t totally and wholeheartedly come to terms with the fact of your stark
and utter triviality, that you may be no more than a negligible blip that can be
considered as well as to never have existed when rounding off for the macrocosmic scheme.
Yet you exist, you do perceive that you’re doubtlessly alive. In a sense, this
confinement to a single perspective and the lack of pondering upon your right place in
the arrangement of life, isn’t that the root of all this self-admiration and egocentrism?
Well I would like to think so. I think too that this has the effect on us to assume that
our actions are somehow right and will fall into place like the jigsaw of some grand
quest, and think that they were meant to be. When someone becomes truly convinced of
that, that’s where the last lights of empathy dies away. And predeterminism seems to play
well with this framework of thought.

How so reamed must be their hole by their head that they read each and every moment with
so much certainty and convince themselves anything that happens as to be somehow someway
about them. The wins or spoils of others, about them. Some person at the lowest point of
their life, about them. Some bird moving that way or some rock flying through space this
way, about them. And poetically, they are the most dumb and blind to the value that
everything around them holds (for them), everything that happens to them, their pedestals
for themselves so high they miss the transitoriness of moments and how they evanesce away
beneath the eye. They just can’t grasp the permanence of impermanence, they are
incapable. They harbour a complex of superiority over certainties that aren’t there.
They see that aren’t and do not that they well should. But one day, if they’re wise
enough, their conceit will fall short and mirrors will shatter. The cataract will ever
so little be undone.

> Whereas the Blind may see \
> the Sighted may not.
