title: 'Sooner We Accept We’re Lost / Sooner We Can Find Our Way'

---

There’s a one-window cell. The world outside is alight. The four walls of our limits
constrict us. We wallow in our despair.

Our thoughts are biased that we tend to underestimate the extremeties of the bad. We’ve
always underestimated the human capability for apathy and hurt and hatred and credulity.
When it’s concentrated in the hands of few such capable freaks, powerful in the
capitalist and political sense, what ensue are bare transgressions on our collective
conscience. Yet still you can believe and hope for the contrary. But belief is truthless
and hope is a toy one gets tired of.

In the absence of empathy, all wit and cunning becomes malignant, it favours
self-preservation, greed, and narcissism. We’ve come this far because we’ve stood
together, beside each other, and because of that alone. Every visionary and magnate
stands on the shoulder of giants known or forgotten. And here it’s perhaps needless to
say that we shouldn’t allow excessive power vested in people brought up cushioned from
the grassroots facts of the nature of human condition. \
Of the torment of hunger. \
Of dreams decayed by circumstance. \
Of the uncertainty of every tomorrow. \
Of the ever-strangling noose of debt. \
Of the ruins of bomb blasts. \
Of not having a corpse to bury. \
Of blood spilt by the divisive politics of hatred taught.

And yet here we’ve a trillionare who cannot articulate his own sentences, speech as
mushed as days-old compost. We’re blissfully oblivious of the implications of all this
because they (the corporate pigs) are pouring their efforts into pacifying critical
thought at nascence with an infinite slop of content, algorithmically curated to capture
(and, of course, divert) attention.

The modern world is a bleak disconcerting world. Build a ballroom to distract the people
from a senseless war. Blame AI when little girls are blown into pieces. Observe warcrimes
elsewhere if it doesn’t do our interests. And justifying genocide? Some acts are simply
transgressions too violent, truly detestable, so unjustifiably cruel: we name them
crimes against humanity. And doesn’t it enrage you they’re carried out like routine in
Ukraine and Palestine? To butcher men and women, to put bullets in children, to murder
those running to save them, over but differences in interpretations and abstract notions,
how lost are we? To deem others inhuman for differing politics, to exploit disasters for
political narrative, where in what darkness are we? How have we sunk.  There’s no panacea
to this. We can do nothing else than perhaps learn and educate. Raise our voices, beat
the iron when it’s hot. All the while always remembering that the people are the state,
the state is because we are, and no man at the head is inherently divine nor superior, but
merely chosen, that is all.

The modern world is a bleak disconcerting world. We’re supposed to convince ourselves
that when they cut down 15 million trees across the Great Nicobar Island they’re doing
development. Over the bodies of the dead despairing Shompen people. In a world already at
the brink of climate catastrophe by the very same, to leave debris of half a world to our
posterity. That when they mine into the face of more than 300 species of coral reefs and
already-endangered whales off American Samoa they’re doing it for the common prosperity
of a country. Wiping out inhabitants of the water that precede us by millions of years.
Plunging robotic apparatus into the seafloor, in a handful minutes killing coral reefs
that took unfathomable millennia to grow that supports a quarter of marine life.

When there remains no more to be developed, they’ll have banged their heads into the
finiteness of our intricately-woven world and realize they’re not exempt from the chain
of causality, they’ll find themselves to have overplayed their hand, because the nature
of greed is this: the more you have the more and more you lust.

The contemporary idea of economic growth is a disease, a vile cancer of sorts. In what
was a sustained cyclic world where life had existed for hundreds of millions of years,
we’ve become collectively the tumour of Earth, a parasite that siphons off to itself all
that is inherited by every life, tiny and large, marine and terrestrial, hostile and
mansuete, all to feed our capitalist consumerist dystopian joke. I think we’ve come to
think of ourselves as too important in the scheme of things. How misconceived.

We’re like the fool sawing off the branch he sits upon. We’re like the man falling from a
50-storeyed building chanting to himself “so far, so good, so far, so good.”

If we sit with ourselves and think for a moment: doesn’t being the vessels of deeper
thoughts and broader imagination that we are, ordain us by the very process of being,
with a responsibility? Towards each of ourselves and to every other living thing? A
responsibility in recognizing that we’re all in this together?
